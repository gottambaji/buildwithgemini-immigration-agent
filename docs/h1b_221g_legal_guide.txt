# H-1B Visa & 221(g) Administrative Processing Legal Guide

## What is 221(g) Administrative Processing?
Section 221(g) of the United States Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) permits a U.S. Consular Officer to suspend processing of a visa application when additional documentation or background checks are required before a final visa decision can be rendered.

## Common 221(g) Scenarios for H-1B Applicants:
1. Client Letter & Vendor Chain Verification: Third-party placement candidates requested to supply End-Client Letters, Master Services Agreements (MSA), and Statements of Work (SOW).
2. TAL (Technology Alert List): STEM applicants in sensitive research or engineering fields undergoing security advisory opinion checks.
3. Employer Financial & Tax Records: Requests for employer W-2 rosters, tax returns, or office lease agreements.

## Legal Advice & Lawyer Assistance:
- Attorney Interventions: Licensed immigration attorneys can submit formal legal briefs and employer documentation directly to consular legal posts.
- Expedite Requests: If 221(g) processing exceeds 60 days causing severe financial loss or urgent humanitarian need, an immigration attorney can file a congressional inquiry or emergency expedite motion with the Department of State.
- Representation Contact: Non-immigrant applicants encountering 221(g) refusals or RFEs should request a direct attorney review.
